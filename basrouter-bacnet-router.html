// router or gateway

A router passes BACnet; it does not translate

⇄

Router

A BACnet router joins BACnet networks — BACnet/IP, BACnet Ethernet, MS/TP — at the network layer. Contemporary Controls puts it plainly: their routers only pass messages, with no knowledge of what they contain. The devices behind it keep their own device instances and objects.

⚙

Gateway

A gateway speaks one protocol on one side and another on the other — Modbus to BACnet, say — and builds new objects for what it reads. That is a different job; see Modbus to BACnet gateway.

🔍

Bring a client

The router finds and manages nothing by itself: a BACnet client on the IP side discovers the MS/TP devices and reads them. Its own Device object is all it holds — it does not keep the other devices' properties.

Bias and termination inside

The BASRT-B ships with bias (604 Ω up and down) and termination (130 Ω) applied by three jumpers under the cover. At the end of a segment leave them; in the middle remove all three and terminate the far end with 120 Ω. A segment takes 31 full-load devices.

⏚

Connect SC

The MS/TP port is isolated: its SC pin must go to the trunk's signal common, or communication fails. With non-isolated two-wire devices, SC goes to their supply common.

🌐

Other subnets: BBMD

On another IP subnet than the BMS, enable BBMD on the Advanced page and list every other BBMD in its table. Do not enable it when it is not needed — Contemporary Controls warns it impairs traffic. A BASrouter can take foreign-device registrations but cannot register as one. More: BBMD and foreign device.

// the rule that bites

Found, but never answered

📡

A routed I-Am

When an MS/TP device answers Who-Is through the router, its I-Am comes from the router's IP address and carries the device's network (2001) and MS/TP MAC in the message's network header. That pair is its address now.

⚠

Reading from the router

Contemporary Controls' troubleshooting note describes clients that discover MS/TP devices via the router, then ignore the routing information and try to read the device's properties from the router itself — which holds only its own. The device shows up in the list and every read times out.

✅

Network and MAC on every request

A request to a device behind a router has to carry its network and MAC as the destination. The router's status page gives it away otherwise: far more BACnet/IP packets than MS/TP ones means the client is not using the routing.

BACManager reads devices behind a router from version 1.4.6: the device list shows them at the router's IP with net 2001, and every read and write carries the device's network and MAC. Tested against a simulated BASrouter with an ABB drive, a Belimo valve and a Lochinvar boiler on MS/TP network 2001: each answered with its own name and values, and the dashboard wrote to the drive and the valve through the router. Without the change, version 1.4.5 finds devices like these and times out on every read of them.

// questions

BASrouter — FAQ

What is the difference between a BACnet router and a gateway?+
A router joins BACnet networks — MS/TP and BACnet/IP — and passes BACnet messages between them unchanged; the devices behind it keep their own objects. A gateway translates another protocol, such as Modbus, into BACnet objects of its own.
What is the BASrouter's default IP address?+
192.168.92.68 with a 24-bit mask and gateway 192.168.92.1, set as fixed. Give the PC an address such as 192.168.92.69 to reach its web page.
What network numbers does the BASrouter use?+
BACnet/IP network 1 and MS/TP network 2001 from the factory. Every MS/TP segment needs its own number across the whole internetwork, so a second router must get another MS/TP network number.
Why can I see MS/TP devices through the router but not read them?+
The client is reading them from the router's own address instead of routing to them. A request must carry the device's network number and MS/TP MAC; the router does not hold other devices' properties.
Do I need to set the BASrouter's device instance?+
Yes. It is 0 from the factory, and every BACnet device in the internetwork needs a unique instance.
Should the BASrouter terminate the MS/TP bus?+
Only at the end of a segment. It ships with bias and termination jumpers in; in the middle of a segment remove all three and terminate the far end with 120 Ω.
